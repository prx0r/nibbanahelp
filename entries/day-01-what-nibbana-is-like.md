# What nibbana is like

Some alien process with stamina bar inside head.

Nibbana is literally just nothing.

Imagine every morning you go on your phone. Then, you decide you don't want to go on your phone in the mornings anymore. So then in the mornings, you feel the pull to go on your phone, but you resist. After one month, you wake up in peace — the thought of going on your phone isn't appealing.

This is what is going on with nibbana, but applied to all mental states.

It is when I sit and there is nothing that drags attention outwards, catches it, pulls it. It is seeing clearly: the anything that does catch it is only temporary, self-liberating.

By doing nothing, you are practising. Don't even watch closely, don't attend to experience, just leave it alone.

*(20 second pause)*

So what is it that actually gets caught?

Luckily the buddha figured it out: hatred, greed, delusion.

But these aren't just when you don't like the server at a cafe, when you want more cake, or believing that eating cake will solve your problems. They refer to categories of behaviour — imagine them as roots or even entities. When you delight in eating the cake, that is strengthening the power of the greed entity.

*(here we have green entity visual)*

---

Buddha is basically saying that these entities are governing your behaviour, and they demand to be fed — if not, they will make you feel bad.

I think the correct approach is imagining them like coals. You can pour water on them again and again but it's just going to create hot steam, making you suffer even more — actually making them warmer, increasing their power.

So how do you make hot coals cool? You leave them alone.

However it's a bit more complex than this, because greed isn't coal — it's a complex pattern of behaviour in the psyche. So immediately abandoning it might be disturbing. A nicer way is replacement. Just as if you are trying to quit smoking, you might repeat the ritual yet only have a coffee. You swap the harmful thing for the less harmful thing that still satisfies the craving.

It's like: imagine ordering and eating a whole slice of cake = 1. Greed is anything that would make you want to next time order more than 1, and non-greed the opposite.

Instead of eating a whole slice of cake, maybe I eat 3 quarters, or even just give a chunk to my friend.

*(pause 10 secs)*

Now next time I have a slice of cake I don't eat it on autopilot. This might turn into having half a slice. Maybe I order a flapjack instead. Maybe even a banana. Then maybe I order nothing at all. And stop showing up at the cafe.

Because you weren't actually hungry.
