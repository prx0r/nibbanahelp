## What this book is

Anthropological-historical argument that the **tantric body is constructed, not discovered** — tradition is *inscribed* on the body through ritual, yoga, and reading. Core term: **entextualisation of the body** — the body becomes the text.

Illustrated across:
- **Pāñcarātra** (Jayākhya-saṃhitā) — fullest concrete nyāsa example + full appendix translation
- **Śaiva Siddhānta** — tattva hierarchy, six paths (incl. varṇa), initiation
- **Kashmir Trika / Pratyabhijñā** — Abhinavagupta, Kṣemarāja, aham, ecstatic tantra

## 1. Entextualisation — why the body takes text

### Core thesis

> "the tantric body becomes inscribed by the text. What we might call an **entextualisation of the body** occurs in tantric traditions that is specific yet allows a divergence of views and practices." (Intro)

> "in its medieval Indian context the tantric body is **not a given that is discovered but a process that is constructed** through dedicated effort over years of practice. The centres of power or cakras… can be best understood in terms of entextualisation, the body inscribed as the text." (Intro)

> "The tantric body is encoded in tradition-specific and text-specific ways. The practitioner inscribes the body through ritual and forms of interiority or asceticism, and so writes the tradition onto the body. Such transformative practices are intended to create the body as divine." (theory statement)

> "the entextualisation of the body in the tantric traditions is the entextualisation of **specific texts**, written in specific times and places."

### Two fundamental principles

1. Hierarchical emanationist cosmology (lower emerges from higher)
2. **Body recapitulates the cosmos** — body becomes a cosmography, a writing of the cosmos → this is entextualisation

### Variable indexicality

The everyday first-person "I" is ritually expanded into the textual "I" (**aham**). Subjectivity is tradition-formed, not a free-floating individual.

### Why this matters for deitybody

Installing phonemes on the body is coherent **as tradition-inscription practice**, not as biomedical anatomy. Month 1 Mātṛkā / Month 2 Mālinī maps are **text-specific entextualisation**.

This is scholarly backup for AGENTS.md lock: provenance SOURCE_ATTESTED vs PEDAGOGICAL.

## 2. Nyāsa — definition + process template

### Etymology

> "The process of imposing mantras on the body is called **nyāsa**, from the verbal root *ny* plus *as*, to put or cast down, within whose semantic range is to place something in a picture, to paint and depict. The practitioner touches the requisite part of the body and recites the correct mantra."

### Process skeleton (shared across traditions — process invariance)

```text
purification (snāna / bhūtaśuddhi)
  → divinisation of body through nyāsa (touch + mantra on body parts)
    → mental / internal worship
      → external worship
```

> "With this ritual sequence we are presented with an excellent example of the way the body becomes the text… The practitioner imposes deities as mantras upon his body and these mantras and deities are **text- and tradition-specific**."

### Jayākhya pattern (Pāñcarātra — comparison, not our install map)

1. Begin with **hands** — root mantra + form mantra on fingers (thumb first)
2. Place deity-mantras throughout body: head, eyes, ears, mouth, shoulders, hands, buttocks, heart, back, navel, hips, knees, feet
3. Appendix ch.11: "Only through the imposition of mantras can he become equal to the God of Gods."
4. Sequence note: "In succession, beginning with **na** and ending with **ha** there are **twenty-two syllables**" — **not** the 50-phoneme Mātṛkā

### Śaiva Siddhānta initiation

> "the master installs in the body of the disciple the totality of the cosmos… the five kalās are established by the master in the body and transferred on to the cord through nyāsa"

### Trika daily rite (Flood)

> "purification of the body, the divinisation of the body through nyāsa, mental worship and external worship"
> After nyāsa: visualise trident maṇḍala along body axis; Parā, Parāparā, Aparā at prongs above crown

### Process invariance vs content particularity

> "the pattern of ritual remains constant, but is filled out with text- and tradition-specific content, especially the mantras."

**For deitybody:** our nyāsa uses **Mātṛkā/Mālinī phonemes** (MV/TĀ), not Jayākhya deity-mantras. Same process, different content. Do not mix rites across texts (Flood: Rauravāgama explicitly forbids mixing).

## 3. Mantra as embodied / sonic form of deity

> "as mudrā is the expression of the deity in the body through gesture, so **mantra is the sonic form of the god**."

> "as the master enlivens the mantra… so the mantravīrya is internalised by the practitioner. Through mantra his body is brought to life as the divine body; **the repetition of mantra (japa) is clearly an entextualisation of the body**."

> "Repeating mantras is entextualising the body."

> "the real source [of mantra] is pure consciousness, absolute subjectivity (**aham**), which is the greatest mantra."

Sadāśiva mantra-body (Abhinavagupta via Flood): Īśāna, Tatpuruṣa, Aghora, Vāmadeva, Sadyojāta — scripture source = mantra body of Śiva.

**For deitybody:** japa / phoneme repetition = body-formation. Primary visual object = Devanāgarī glyph (text inscribed). Voice = install.

## 4. Sound, phonemes, aham — cosmological frame (not install maps)

### Inner nāda → mouth → varṇatva

> "divine sound… cosmic sound manifests and resonates in all the levels of the cosmos… through supreme and subtle to gross levels where it is expressed as mantra."
> "an inner sound which… **moves up through the body to the mouth** and takes on the quality of formulated sound (**varṇatva**) as a word (śabda)."

### aham contains the alphabet

Abhinavagupta via Flood (TĀ):

> "This 'I' contains within itself the totality of manifestation, as indicated by the very word **aham** in so far as it contains the **first phoneme** of the Sanskrit alphabet **a**… and ends with **m**, regarded as the 'drop' or 'dot', the **bindu**…"
> TĀ: "The flowing forth [of the cosmos] whose nature is energy begins with the incomparable (**a**) and ends with **ha**…"
> "a represents the father… ha is the mother… aspirate or visarga… finally retrieve the condition of the incomparable (anuttara) with the anusvara (**ṃ**) or bindu."

Kuṇḍalinī link: "links Kuṇḍalinī to the force of the syllable **ha** in the mantra and the concept of **aham**."

### Kṣemarāja — śakticakra = letters a to kṣa

> "Her form is the eternally enunciated great mantra… pregnant with the complete circle of powers (śakticakra) whose form [comprises the letters] **from a to kṣa**."
> "The goddess gives birth… to the cosmos as the circle of powers, envisaged as the **letters of the Sanskrit alphabet**."
> Four levels of speech: parā · paśyantī · madhyamā · vaikharī

Footnote: Sanderson, "The Visualisation of the Deities of the Trika" — alphabet deities of Trika.

### Six paths — varṇa as path of sound

| Path of Sound (vācaka) | Path of Objects (vācya) |
|------------------------|-------------------------|
| varṇa (phonemes) → mantra → pada (words) | kalā → tattva → bhuvana |

> "for the monistic Śaivas these are manifestations of consciousness paired in a hierarchical sequence, kalā with varṇa, tattva with mantra, and bhuvana with pada"

### Later cakra-letters — dated late

> "By the time of the later Kaulism… and medieval Haṭha yoga… cakra refers to points or lotuses with varying numbers of petals, **specific letters of the alphabet and colours**, located along the central axis of the body."

Flood/Sanderson: six-cakra + three-nāḍī model is **post-eleventh century**. Early Trika variety: six seasons, five knots, nine/eleven wheels, etc.

**For deitybody:** Flood gives the **why phonemes can be deities/cosmography** frame. He does **not** give Mātṛkā/Mālinī body maps. Those stay in MV ch.3 / TĀ 15 / PTv.

## 5. Breath in inscription context

- Cakras connected by nāḍīs along which prāṇa flows; control via yogic/tantric practice
- Śaiva Siddhānta: ten breaths; apāna digestion/excretion; udāna eye movement
- Netra-tantra: visualise Śakti in central breath between inhalation and exhalation; identify Śakti with mantra
- Abhinavagupta: kuṇḍalinī as energy + as breath (prāṇakuṇḍalinkā); links body–breath–pleasure/pain; kuṇḍalinī + ha-syllable + aham
- Jayākhya bhūtaśuddhi: body-part dissolves into mantra → element → **released on inhale / retained / exhaled**; sound-element emitted through crown aperture
- Kula: visualise deities gratified by nectar of ingoing breath

**For deitybody:** supports natural breath + phonation-lengthened exhale. Flood never teaches 1:4:2 — consistent with TĀ 4.91 lock.

## 6. Reading / smṛti as body-forming (Bruno bridge)

> "the receiver of the Tantra… internalises the text through a process of identification which usually involves ritual enactment… The reader does not simply interpret; **the text makes claims upon the initiated reader**."

> "the speech agent retrieves the text back into the living matrix of speech through meaningful acts of reading and performance, **through encoding the body with the text**."

> "what constitutes religious reading is not the quality of attention but the **indwelling of the subject in the text and the text in the subject**."

> "The verb **smṛ**, 'to remember', is often used for visualisation… bringing to mind and evoking the forms of tradition… The tāntrika lives within 'memory'… as an edifice of a ritual–visual symbolic order that his body is within and that is also within his body."

**Bruno bridge (PEDAGOGICAL, not Flood):** memory-wheel vividness as a modern smṛti technology for encoding dual-coordinate phoneme objects. Flood does not mention Bruno.

## 7. Trika / Abhinavagupta interpretive layer

- Abhinavagupta c. 975–1025 CE; royal patronage; *Tantrāloka* = Illumination of the Tantras
- Highest revelation: **Mālinīvijayottaratantra**; Abhinavagupta wrote ślokavṛttika on it; TĀ is practical manual; Tantrasāra = summary
- "ecstatic tantra" — spontaneous expansion of consciousness as ground of being
- TĀ defines aham as "reflexive awareness of the omnipresent in the non-duality of Śiva and Śakti… supreme and cosmic emission within which all is contained"
- Parātrikā (Rudrayāmala verses) commentary — absolute subject as source of appearance; "uninterrupted continuity"
- Three goddesses: **Parā · Parāparā · Aparā** — forms of language/address
- **Important Trika vs Siddhānta difference:** for Trika, daily rite / entextualisation of body is **not manipulation of material substance but action within consciousness** — ritual must be understood in terms of cognition and knowledge
- TĀ ch.29 = clearest description of kula rite
- Sanderson caveat Flood reports: MVT root text is dualist; Abhinavagupta projects monism from Krama/Pratyabhijñā onto it

**For deitybody:** practice is **action within consciousness** for non-dual Trika — glyph + locus + attention, not muscle memory alone. Dual-coordinate objects are consciousness-operations, not only drills.
