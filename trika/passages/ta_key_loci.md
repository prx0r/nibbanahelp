## TĀ 4.91 — prāṇāyāma without torment

Volume context (`ahnika-04.txt` ~7331–7334), Virāvali stanza cited in Abhinavagupta:

```text
prāpte ca dvādaśe bhāge jīvāditye svabodhake |
mokṣaḥ sa eva kathitaḥ prāṇāyāmo nirarthakaḥ | 90 ||
prāṇāyāmo na kartavyaḥ śarīraṁ yena pīḍyate |
rahasyaṁ vetti yo yatra sa muktaḥ sa ca mocakaḥ || 91 ||
```

Sense of 91: **prāṇāyāma is not to be practised in a manner that torments/distresses the body.** (90 also notes prāṇāyāma becomes pointless once the twelfth part / self-luminous jīvāditya is realized — context is high attainment, not anti-prāṇāyāma polemic.)

## PTv — Mālinī at madhyamā-vāc

From TĀ 15 appendix B (volume ahnika-15.txt ~5917–5965):

> Over half of Abhinava’s commentary on the Parātrīśikā is dedicated to the first four of its thirty-six verses. He structures it around the four levels of Speech… Abhinava expounds the **Mālinī order of the alphabet** in the context of explaining the **Middle Speech (madhyamā)**.

> According to Abhinava, **Mālinī is the alphabet in which the order of the letters, seeds and matrices, are mixed up**.
